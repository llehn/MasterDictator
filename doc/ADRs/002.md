# What a commit message carries

## Decision

A subject line, in the imperative, saying what the change does.

A body only when it carries something the diff cannot: why the change was made, or a measurement
that can be re-checked. A few lines at most.

No attribution trailers.

## Rationale

Retelling the mechanism is redundant — the code and its comments are right there, and they stay
correct while a message cannot be edited.

A measurement earns its line because it can be verified by someone who doubts it: "21 seconds down
to 1" is a claim with a test attached.

Attribution here would be constant on every commit, since every one is written the same way by the
same pair, and a link to the conversation that produced a change resolves for one person on one
account rather than for the repository.

## Consequences

No attribution trailer, no link to a session, at most three lines, at most two hundred characters.
Kept as a rule rather than a convention because the convention was not kept — messages arrived
retelling the mechanism at length, and carrying both trailers.

Three lines means a subject, a blank line, and one line of body. A change whose reason does not fit
in that line is one whose reason belongs in a comment beside the code, where it stays correct.
